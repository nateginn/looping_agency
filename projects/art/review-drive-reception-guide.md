# Google Review Drive — Front Desk Guide

**Greeley vs Denver · Ends September 1** · Prize: $100 to the winning location

---

## How we're scoring

**Total Google reviews collected — any star rating.**

Not "most 5-star reviews." That matters: if we counted only 5-stars, the natural move is to
ask only the patients we think are happy. That's called review gating, and Google can remove
every review a profile has for it. One business went from hundreds of reviews to 12 and
hadn't recovered a year later.

**So: ask everyone, the same way, every time.** A 4-star review counts exactly the same as a
5-star one for this competition. A profile with a few 4s also looks more trustworthy to
patients than a wall of perfect 5s.

---

## The ask

**When:** the moment the visit ends, at checkout. Not later, not by email a week after.
Response rates collapse with every hour that passes.

**How:** hand them the QR code or send the link while they're still standing there.

**What to say** — natural, not scripted:

> "If you've got a minute, a quick Google review really helps other people in
> [Greeley/Denver] find us. I can text you the link right now if that's easier."

That's it. No pressure, no follow-up speech. If they say no, say "no problem at all" and
move on — genuinely. A patient who feels pushed leaves a worse review than one who was never
asked.

**One SMS follow-up**, within 24 hours, if they didn't do it at checkout. **One.** Not a
sequence, not a reminder every day.

**Ask for a photo too.** "A photo of the clinic or your visit helps too, if you're up for
it." Photos attached to reviews carry location data that Google reads as a genuine local
signal — they're worth more than text alone.

---

## If they ask "what should I write?"

This comes up often, and the answer matters. **Never tell them what to say.** But you can
describe *the kinds of detail that help other patients decide*:

> "Whatever's honest. People usually find it helpful when you mention which service
> you came in for and what you came in with."

Why: Google reads the words in reviews and uses them to decide which searches we show up
for. A review saying *"came in for sciatica after a car accident, the Greeley team sorted
it out"* helps us far more than *"great place!"* — because those are the words patients
actually search.

**Things patients might naturally mention** — offer as examples only if asked, never as a
list to copy:

**Location** — Greeley, Windsor, Evans, Milliken, Severance, Eaton
**Location** — Denver, Jefferson Park, Highland, Sloan Lake, Union Station, Auraria

**Service** — chiropractic, physical therapy, massage, acupuncture, dry needling,
shockwave therapy, spinal decompression *(Denver only)*

**What brought them in** — back pain, neck pain, sciatica, herniated disc, shoulder pain,
car accident or auto injury, work injury, plantar fasciitis, sports injury, headaches

**Who they saw** — their provider by name

If they mention two or three of those in their own words, that's ideal. If they write
"great staff, felt better," that's still a win. **Their words, their choice, always.**

## Never do these

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| **Offer patients anything for a review** — discount, gift card, drawing entry | Against Google policy. Reviews get removed, profile can be actioned. The $100 is for staff, never for patients. |
| **Ask only the happy ones** | Review gating. Can wipe every review on the profile. |
| **Write a review for a patient, or on their device** | Fraudulent. Detectable. |
| **Tell them what to write, or hand them a script** | Google detects patterned reviews. Also just dishonest. |
| **Ask the same patient repeatedly** | One ask, one follow-up. That's the limit. |

## Responding to reviews — HIPAA

We respond to every review, ideally within 24–48 hours. Response rate and speed are both
things Google measures.

**But this is a medical practice, and a review response is public.**

**Never confirm someone is a patient. Never mention their condition, treatment, or visit —
even if they mentioned it themselves in the review.** Them disclosing it publicly does not
give us permission to confirm it.

❌ *"Thanks Sarah! Glad your back is feeling better after your adjustments."*
✅ *"Thank you for the kind words — we appreciate you taking the time."*

**For a negative review**, the instinct is to correct the record. Don't — you cannot
discuss any specifics publicly. Take it offline:

> "Thank you for the feedback. We take these concerns seriously — please contact our
> office directly so we can look into it."

Then tell the office manager. Do not debate it in public.
