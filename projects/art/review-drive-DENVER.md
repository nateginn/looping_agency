## How we're scoring

**Every review counts — any star rating.**

Not just 5-stars. If we only counted 5-stars, we'd start asking only the patients we think
are happy — and that's called review gating. Google can remove **every review on the
profile** for it. One business dropped from hundreds of reviews to 12.

So we ask everyone, the same way, every time. A 4-star review scores the same as a 5-star.

## The ask — at checkout, every patient

**Say something like:**

> "If you've got a minute, a quick Google review really helps other people in Denver
> find us. I can text you the link right now if that's easier."

Hand them the QR code or text the link **while they're still standing here.** Response
rates fall off fast once they leave.

If they say no — "no problem at all," and move on. Genuinely. A patient who felt pushed
writes a worse review than one who was never asked.

**One text follow-up**, within 24 hours, if they didn't do it at checkout. **One only.**

**Ask for a photo too:** *"A photo helps as well, if you're up for it."* Photos carry
location data Google reads as a genuine local signal.

## If they ask "what should I write?"

**Never tell them what to say.** But you can describe what helps other patients:

> "Whatever's honest. People usually find it helpful when you mention which service you
> came in for and what you came in with."

Google reads the words in reviews to decide which searches we appear for. *"Came in for
sciatica after a car accident, the Denver team sorted it out"* helps far more than
*"great place!"* — those are the words patients actually search.

**Only if they ask for examples:**

- **Where:** Denver, Jefferson Park, Highland, Sloan Lake, Union Station, Auraria,
  Villa Park
- **Service:** chiropractic, physical therapy, massage, acupuncture, dry needling,
  shockwave therapy, **spinal decompression** *(Denver only — worth mentioning)*
- **What brought them in:** back pain, neck pain, sciatica, herniated disc, shoulder pain,
  car accident, work injury, plantar fasciitis, sports injury, headaches
- **Who they saw:** their provider's name

**Their words, their choice. Always.**

## Never do these

| ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Offer a patient anything for a review | Against Google policy. Reviews get removed. **The $100 is for staff, never patients.** |
| Ask only the happy ones | Review gating — can wipe every review we have |
| Write it for them, or on their phone | Fraudulent and detectable |
| Hand them a script | Google detects patterned reviews |
| Ask the same patient twice | One ask, one follow-up. That's it. |

## Responding to reviews — HIPAA

**A review response is public. Never confirm someone is a patient. Never mention their
condition, treatment, or visit — even if they mentioned it themselves.**

❌ *"Thanks Sarah! Glad your back feels better after your adjustments."*
✅ *"Thank you for the kind words — we appreciate you taking the time."*

**Negative review?** Don't explain or correct anything publicly. Post this, then tell the
office manager:

> "Thank you for the feedback. We take these concerns seriously — please contact our
> office directly so we can look into it."
